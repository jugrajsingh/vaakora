# Changelog

This project is built on [Handy](https://github.com/cjpais/Handy) v0.10.1 and documents its own changes on top of it, following [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/). Changes merged in from upstream are documented in upstream's own release notes.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

Read aloud (text to speech)

- A Read aloud shortcut (default Super+Alt+R, changeable under General) reads the text you last copied. It takes the clipboard with `wl-paste` on Wayland and `xclip` on X11. A popup opens and starts reading at once. Pressing the shortcut again while the popup is open reads the new clipboard text from the start in place of the current text, and stops reading and closes the popup only when the clipboard text is the same as the popup's text (ignoring line endings and surrounding whitespace) or the clipboard is empty or not text.
- The popup shows the text with the sentence being read highlighted, and the highlight follows the audio. While a read is playing or paused, clicking or pasting no longer stops it; use Edit text. After a read finishes or fails the text stays in the reading view and becomes editable when you click it. It shows elapsed and total time with a progress bar, and Play, Pause and Stop buttons. A Read clipboard button next to Play replaces the text with the clipboard text and reads it from the start, even when the text is the same. An empty clipboard, or one that is not text, leaves the popup as it is. Esc and the close button close the popup and Ctrl+Enter plays or pauses.
- Errors appear in a banner in the popup. When the chosen model is not downloaded, the banner links to the Text-to-speech Models page. If the chosen output device cannot be opened, reading falls back to the default output device and the popup says so. If a dictation is still pasting when you press the shortcut, a notice asks you to try again, and if the voice worker is not ready a notice says reading is unavailable.
- Eight downloadable voice models: Pocket TTS (English, the default; about 296 MB, 27 voices), Supertonic 3 (about 401 MB, 10 voices), Kokoro-82M INT8 (about 107 MB, 28 voices), Paradee-8M INT8 (about 8.5 MB, 4 voices), Phonon 31M English (about 79 MB, 26 voices) and Indic Tiny TTS for Indian English, Hindi and Punjabi (about 91 MB each, 2 voices each). Each file is pinned to a Hugging Face revision with a fixed size and SHA-256, and downloads are verified and resumable.
- Paradee-8M v1.2, a small and fast English voice model distilled from Kokoro, with four voices (two American, two British). It runs on the CPU only, about 55 times faster than real time at normal speed on the machine the ratings were measured on, and its card credits its author and Kokoro (Apache-2.0). Text with nothing to pronounce reads as a tenth of a second of silence.
- Phonon 31m English, a small English voice model by Gradium built on Pocket TTS and the Mimi codec, with 26 voices (a default voice plus 25 American and British voices). It runs on the CPU only, about 16 times faster than real time at normal speed on the machine the ratings were measured on, and its card credits Gradium (Apache-2.0), Pocket TTS and the Mimi codec by Kyutai (CC-BY-4.0), and the voice set (Apache-2.0). Text with nothing to pronounce, such as only punctuation, dashes or emoji, reads as a tenth of a second of silence.
- Indic Tiny TTS, small voice models by Sulabh Katiyar for Indian English, Hindi and Punjabi (Gurmukhi script), each with a female and a male voice. They run on the CPU, about 20 (Punjabi) to 48 (English) times faster than real time at normal speed on the machine the ratings were measured on, and on an Intel GPU through OpenVINO; there is no NPU path. Hindi and Punjabi spell English words and abbreviations letter by letter in their own script, and name path symbols in it (for example slash, dot and underscore), so technical text such as `README.md` is read and not dropped. Text with nothing to pronounce reads as a tenth of a second of silence. Each card credits the model (CC-BY-4.0), the voice data (LIMMITS 2024 for English, the AI4Bharat Rasa dataset for Hindi and Punjabi, CC-BY-4.0) and the Vocos vocoder by Charactr Inc. (MIT), and the English card notes that the source does not document speaker consent for release.
- Pocket voices are gated on Hugging Face. Accept the voice terms there, then enter a Hugging Face token in the model card or under Advanced. The token is kept in memory only and is cleared when the app restarts. The `HF_TOKEN` environment variable is also honoured.
- Each model runs on the CPU through ONNX Runtime, or on an Intel GPU or NPU through OpenVINO (Paradee-8M and Phonon run on the CPU only, and Indic Tiny has no NPU path). The Run on control on each downloaded model's card offers Auto, CPU, GPU and NPU, listing only devices that model supports on this machine. Auto picks the best available device and falls back when one fails to prepare. The cards show Quality and Speed ratings, a Recommended badge, and a progress line while a device prepares. The ratings ship with the app and were measured on one machine (see Platform support).
- Speed from 0.5x to 2.0x and pitch from -6 to +6 semitones, applied by time stretching with a single pass over the audio. A speed or pitch change applies from the next sentence. Pause lengths after punctuation scale with the speed.
- Reading is streamed. Several sentences are synthesized in parallel (up to three, fewer when memory is short) while playback streams ahead with a bounded read-ahead. When a model or device synthesizes slower than playback, reading pauses and the popup shows Buffering until enough audio is ready. Silence at the edges of each sentence is trimmed and replaced with a pause that suits the punctuation. Supertonic on the GPU uses six fixed input sizes that are warmed up when the model loads, and on the NPU every model uses fixed input shapes.
- Text is prepared as Markdown, so formatting marks are not spoken, and fenced code blocks are replaced by the words "code block skipped" unless Read code blocks is switched on. Text with very long unbroken runs is still read to the end.
- Technical reading is on by default and speaks paths, file names, extensions, flags, versions and code names the way an engineer would say them. Slashes, underscores, dots and dashes are named, so `tests/lib.sh` is read as "tests slash lib dot S H" and `--dry-run` as "dash dash dry dash run". A leading `~` is read as "home". Names in camelCase or snake_case are split into words. A known extension is read as its letters or name (".md" as "markdown", ".yaml" as "Y A M L"), a `:line` or `:line:column` suffix is read as "at line 684 column 7", each dot of a version is spoken (`v0.10.0` as "version 0 dot 10 dot 0"), and the `src` folder is read as "source". An acronym from the vocabulary is spelled in any letter case when it is a whole word or a whole part of a hyphenated word, so "the cli tool" is read as "the C L I tool" and `cli-tool` as "C L I tool", while words that only contain one, such as "click", "client", "clis" and "decline", are left alone. Ordinary prose, decimals and numbers are left as they are, and so are abbreviations such as "e.g.". Web links are read as "link to <site>".
- Three toggles on the Text-to-speech page control it: Technical reading (on by default), Read full paths (on by default; off reads only the file name of a path) and Read full links (off by default; on reads the site and its path instead of only the site). Read full paths and Read full links apply only while Technical reading is on. Changing any of them while reading applies from the current position without repeating text that was already spoken.
- Pronunciations come from a built-in vocabulary that you can extend. The file `tts-vocabulary.json` in the app data directory is created on first use with no entries and is read again at the start of every read, so an edit applies to the next read without a restart. It can add or override symbols, extensions, terms, acronyms and checked examples. A term can match a whole word, any part of one, a prefix, a suffix or a regular expression. An optional `models` section holds entries for one voice model id that apply only while that model is selected. Every entry of yours wins over every built-in entry, whatever either one's match type: a read uses your entries for one model, then your entries for all voices, then the built-in entries for that model, then the built-in entries for all voices. An optional `disabled` object, at the top level for all voices or inside a `models` section for that model, switches entries off without deleting them: `"disabled": {"mine": {...}, "builtin": {...}}`, each holding lists of `symbols`, `extensions`, `terms` and `acronyms` keys. `mine` switches off your own entry, so the built-in one applies again, and `builtin` switches off a built-in entry, acronyms included, so that text is left to the voice. A top-level `builtin` switch turns that built-in entry off for every voice, including in the built-in `models` sections, so it keeps working when an update moves the entry into a voice's section; one inside a `models` section acts on that section only. An optional `"acronym_style": "capitals"`, at the top level or inside a `models` section, gives that voice acronyms as written capitals ("CLI") instead of spaced letters ("C L I", the default, or `"spaced"`); the same layer order picks the style. A term written as `{"skip": true}` (or the empty string `""`) is not spoken at all. The word before it ends in one punctuation mark: the strongest of its own and those after the skipped terms (`?` and `!`, then `.`, `;`, `:` and `,`), so "Ticket ABC-123, ABC-123. Next one" with `ABC-123` skipped reads "Ticket. Next one." and the sentence ends and pauses as if the mark were written there; the highlight of that word covers the skipped terms. That punctuation never moves back across the pause after a heading, paragraph, list item or code block; a skipped term right after such a pause drops its punctuation, and nothing empty or only punctuation is sent to the voice. Spoken replacements may hold only letters, digits and spaces, including Devanagari and Gurmukhi vowel signs, virama and nukta.
- A vocabulary file that cannot be used never fails or delays a read. Reading falls back to the built-in vocabulary, and a "Vocabulary File Has an Error" warning (notice code `VocabularyInvalid`) names the file and the error, with line and column when known. A file that is not valid JSON or has an unsupported version, an entry that is not valid, text that is not UTF-8, a file over 1 MiB and anything that is not a regular file are all handled this way. The warning is shown once for each distinct error and is cleared by the next successful load.
- `handy-tts --check-vocabulary [file]` runs every example in the built-in vocabulary, or in the built-in vocabulary merged with your file. It prints PASS or FAIL for each example and exits with a failure status if any example fails, so a vocabulary edit can be checked before it is used. An example without a `model` runs against the shared vocabulary and every model that has entries. A model's own `symbols` may change the reading, but any other model entry that changes the reading of a shared example fails it.
- Saving vocabulary changes from the app rewrites `tts-vocabulary.json` in a standard layout (two-space JSON, symbol, extension and switch keys sorted) and keeps every other entry, with terms and acronyms in their file order. The new text goes to a temporary file beside the real one, which then replaces it in one step, through to the target when the file is a symbolic link and with its permissions kept. A save is refused, leaving the file as it was, when the file changed on disk since it was loaded, when it has an error, or when the result would be larger than 1 MiB.
- The Text-to-speech settings page has a Vocabulary tab beside Settings. It lists every reading, built-in and yours, with what is written, what is said, the kind (symbol, extension, term or acronym), the voices it applies to and how it matches. There is one row for each distinct reading: rows with the same written text, kind, spoken words, match options and switch collapse into one, so an acronym that three voices spell the same shows once with three voice chips, and a separate row appears only when a voice really says it differently (for example `/` is read as "slash" by most voices and differently by the Hindi and Punjabi voices, so it has three rows, the shared one marked "except" those two). An "All voices" chip means the row applies to every voice. The Voices picker on a row is a multi-select: adding a voice to your own entry saves the same entry in that voice's section, removing one deletes it there (an all-voices entry becomes one entry per remaining voice), and for a built-in row removing a voice switches it off for that voice and adding it back switches it on. Every change is one save planned from the table as it is when the save runs, so a change queued behind another that took the same voice is refused with a message instead of replacing the other entry; a voice that already has your own different reading of the same entry cannot be added, and a row never loses its last voice. When your entry overrides a built-in one at the same scope, they show as one row: your words marked Yours, the built-in words struck through below as "default:" (with the voices it belongs to when only some of the row's voices override a built-in entry), and Reset to default, which brings the built-in entry back for the voices that override one. Each row has a switch (which switches every voice the row lists, in one save; switching off a built-in row for all voices saves the other readings of that entry that the row does not list, such as the Hindi and Punjabi slash, as your own entries first, because a built-in all-voices switch would otherwise turn those off too), Edit and Listen, which plays the entry's sample with the voice model the entry belongs to or the selected one. Your own rows also have Delete, which asks first. Built-in rows cannot be deleted, only switched off, and editing one saves your own version on top of it. Add entry and Edit take the written text, the kind, what to say or Skip, the match (whole word or anywhere in a word, and case) and, for a new entry or a row on one voice, the voices (all voices or one downloaded model). Above the table, a search box looks in the written and spoken words, and Voice, Kind and Source (Built-in, Yours, or Changed by you, meaning built-in entries you overrode, switched off or shadowed) and Only problems narrow the rows. Under a row the tab says when it never applies: always beaten by a higher-priority entry, unable to match and why, or switched off. When the file changed on disk, the table reloads and asks you to make the edit again; when the file has an error, the tab shows the error and turns editing off.
- The Vocabulary tab has an Add correction button for fixing how a text is read. Type or paste the written text and Handy shows how it is spoken. Edit the words it should say, press Listen to hear them with the selected voice, and see what the edit saves as and who it reaches (for example `.md` read as "dot M D" for every .md file, with a switch to save only the exact text). Choose All voices or one downloaded voice model every time, then Save. An edit that no single rule explains, that changes more than one written word or that the output rules reject shows its reason and cannot be saved. The same scope choice is in the Add entry editor. Each downloaded voice has an acronym style, spaced letters (C L I) or written capitals (CLI), set in the collapsed Acronym style per voice section at the bottom of the Vocabulary tab. A correction for one voice is checked against that voice, and an emptied word can be saved as a skip.
- A correction, or an entry in the Add entry editor, can be checked across voices before saving. For All voices, Play all plays the text on each downloaded voice model in turn (for a correction, the selected text as each voice will read it once the rule is saved; for an entry, its spoken words) at the current speed with that model's chosen or default voice, naming the model as it plays; for one voice, Listen plays that voice. Alongside, each voice says the words and the current speech-to-text model transcribes them, and the box shows "heard right" or "missed: <what was heard>" per voice (lowercase, punctuation ignored, spaced letters joined, so "G P U" matches "GPU"). The check is advisory and never blocks saving: when a voice misses a rule for all voices, you can save for all voices anyway, save only for the voices that passed (one rule per voice) or pick one voice. Changing a match option checks again. An acronym is played and checked the way each voice reads acronyms (written capitals or spaced letters, as set in the Vocabulary tab). Voice models are loaded one at a time for the check, and the speech-to-text model is unloaded afterwards under the unload-immediately setting, unless a dictation or a newer check took it over. The transcription is raw: no custom-word correction, filler removal or post-processing, nothing is pasted and nothing is added to the speech-to-text history. A check is refused while a dictation records or a file transcribes, and a dictation that starts stops a running check and Play all. A voice that is missing or fails reports only its own row.
- The correction box has a microphone button next to Should say. Press it, say how the text should be read, and press it again: the current speech-to-text model transcribes what you said and puts the words in Should say, where you can still edit them. Typing stays the main way to correct a reading. The transcription is raw: no custom-word correction, filler removal or post-processing, nothing is pasted, nothing is added to the speech-to-text history, and no recording overlay or sound is used. The recording goes only to speech-to-text and is never saved or sent anywhere. The microphone is refused while a dictation, a recording or a file transcription runs and while Read aloud is open, and a playing preview stops when it starts. A dictation started while the microphone records or transcribes always wins: the microphone stops and Should say is left as it was. The speech-to-text model is unloaded afterwards under the unload-immediately setting, unless a dictation stopped the microphone.
- Read aloud keeps a history of your reads on this computer, text only and never audio, in its own file `tts-history.db` in the app data directory. Each read is saved once its audio starts playing, and a read that never plays is not saved: the written text, the text actually sent to the voice, which written word became which spoken words, the time, the voice model, the voice and the speed. Reads with Technical reading off are saved too. Playing the same text again is not saved again unless the voice model or voice changed or a setting changed what is said. Two settings, separate from speech-to-text history, control it: how many reads to keep (5 by default, up to 1000) and when to delete them (keep nothing, keep only that many, or also delete reads older than 3 days, 2 weeks or 3 months). Keep nothing, or a limit of 0, saves nothing and empties the history. The rules apply when the app starts, at every save and when either setting changes. A setting change applies to saves still in progress, and Clear history also drops a read that was about to be saved. Clearing the history never touches the vocabulary, and a vocabulary change never alters saved reads. The history is opened, prepared and saved in the background, so it never delays reading or app start, and if it cannot be saved, reading goes on and the failure is logged.
- A History tab on the Text-to-speech page (between Settings and Vocabulary) lists your past reads, newest first, with the time, voice model and voice. The selected read shows each written word with the words that were said for it directly under it, word under word, with changed words tinted and skipped words marked; the spoken words are exactly what was saved, nothing is prepared again. Drag over words to select a chunk, which snaps to whole words, and the correction box opens with that chunk as Selected, the saved spoken words as Spoken as and the read's voice model as the voice the rule is worked out against, so a rule saves and checks across voices like any other correction (Listen plays in the current voice until you choose where the rule applies). The tab also holds the history settings: the limit (0 to 1000, applied when you leave the field or press Enter), the auto-delete choice (Never keep, Keep latest N, After 3 days, After 2 weeks, After 3 months) and Clear history, which asks first and never touches the vocabulary. Changing the limit or the auto-delete choice reloads the list, so reads it removed disappear at once.
- Dictation and reading never overlap. Starting a dictation stops reading and closes the popup. Pressing Read aloud during a dictation ends the recording, lets the dictation finish, then opens the popup.
- The voice engine runs as a separate worker process that is started when needed and unloaded after the existing model unload timeout. It refuses to start with less than 1 GiB of available memory. After three failed starts in a row it stays in a Failed state until a model is selected again.
- Model ids, voice lists and the allowed speed are read from the model catalog instead of lists repeated in each component, and a model with no saved voice reads with its own default voice. A voice list in the catalog replaces voice discovery from the pinned files.
- The ratings measurement commands take `--only <model id>[,<model id>]` to measure chosen models, `--max-load <n>` to refuse to start when the one-minute load average is above `n`, and `--merge <ratings.json>` to replace only the measured models in an existing ratings file and record the measurement date and highest load per model. Devices a model does not offer are recorded as unsupported without starting the worker.
- `handy-tts --synthesize-lines <model> <root> <device> <cache> <lines.json> <out-dir> --speed <s> [--voice <style>]` loads one voice model and writes one WAV per line of an audit file. The `tts_vocabulary_audit` developer example uses it to audit the built-in vocabulary one voice model at a time (any catalog model id): `corpus` writes a short carrier sentence for every built-in entry (an acronym both in capitals and in a hyphenated word), once read with the entry's rule and once with that entry switched off, as the voice would get it without the entry, three runs each; `score` transcribes them with a local speech-to-text model and decides each entry by majority, matching the way the voice check does (lowercase, punctuation ignored, spaced letters joined) against the written text, its letters spelled out or the rule's words (a spoken "dot" the recogniser writes as a dot joined to the next word counts), so an entry is needed, redundant (the raw text already comes through) or a defect (the rule itself does not); entries said only in a script the recogniser cannot write, and entries on a voice that spells both sentences out letter by letter, are marked not audited and stay with that voice. `combine` reads the stored results of several models and proposes a vocabulary file that removes redundant entries, keeps entries every model needs for all voices and moves the others into those models' `models` sections, with a decision table, the examples the proposal would fail and any audited sentence it would read differently. It never changes the built-in vocabulary.
- A Text-to-speech settings page (model, voice with a Preview voice button, speed, pitch, output device, Read code blocks, Technical reading, Read full paths and Read full links) and a Text-to-speech Models page, with the same card design as Transcription Models.
- The tray menu has a Text-to-speech model submenu with a checked list of downloaded models, the worker's status (Unloaded, Starting, Loaded, Stopping or Failed) and an Unload text-to-speech model item that is enabled only while the worker is loaded.
- The RPM bundles the worker and the OpenVINO libraries at `/usr/lib/Handy/tts`, and recommends `intel-compute-runtime`, `intel-level-zero`, `oneapi-level-zero` and `wl-clipboard`. The `HANDY_TTS_WORKER` environment variable overrides the worker path for development.

In-app local post-processing

- New "Local (in-app)" post-processing provider that runs a small language model on the CPU through a bundled llama.cpp `llama-server`. No external service has to be installed or started, and transcripts never leave the machine.
- This build starts `llama-server` as a managed child process. It listens on a private Unix socket inside a directory that only the current user can open (mode 0700) under `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR`, or under the app data directory when that is not set, and it opens no network port. A random API key is generated for each launch and passed through the environment instead of the command line. The app stops the server on exit, and on Linux the kernel terminates it if the app dies without cleaning up.
- The model loads only when needed: it starts in the background when a post-processing recording begins, so recording is never delayed, and it unloads after the existing model unload timeout (a duration, Immediately, or Never).
- Before a model is loaded, its file is checked against its pinned size and SHA-256. A corrupt file is reported with advice to delete and re-download it, and a check that runs past the start deadline falls back to the raw transcript.
- Local post-processing never blocks a dictation on failure. A start deadline of 10 seconds applies, a server that exits unexpectedly is started again on the next dictation, and after three failed starts in a row the model enters an error state. When the last of them was a start timeout, for example under heavy machine load, the first dictation after 5 minutes tries one more start and recovers on its own; after any other failure the error state lasts till a model is selected or deleted, or the app restarts. Every failure falls back to the raw transcript.
- Five downloadable local models, each pinned to a Hugging Face revision with a fixed size and SHA-256: S1-mini by Superwhisper, Quill 0.8B, Quill 2B, Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 and Muesli 2B (all Q4_K_M quantizations). Each model shows its attribution and a link to its model card.
- Models are downloaded and deleted on demand through the verified, resumable downloader. Download progress and errors survive leaving the page, and a stale error is cleared before the next model action.
- S1-mini is driven by Styling (casual, semi-casual, semi-formal, formal), Structure (prose, lists) and Context (general, email) dropdowns.
- Quill, Qwen3 and Muesli models use the selected prompt as their system prompt, and the Prompts group is shown for them. S1-mini uses its own fixed prompt, so the prompt editor is hidden for it with a note. If the selected prompt is missing or empty, post-processing is skipped and the raw transcript is kept.
- Long transcripts are split at sentence boundaries, processed in order and rejoined. The token budget for each chunk and for the reply is estimated per script, so Gurmukhi, Devanagari and other Indic text gets a larger reply budget. A model that still reaches the limit falls back to the raw transcript. Output that loses more than half of the input words is rejected, and think blocks and invisible characters are stripped from model output.
- A live status label (Not loaded, Loading, Ready, Error with the reason) is shown next to the model controls.
- The RPM bundles a static, CPU-only `llama-server` at `/usr/lib/Handy/llm/llama-server`. The `HANDY_LLAMA_SERVER` environment variable overrides the path for development.
- An opt-in integration test (`HANDY_LOCAL_LLM_IT=1`) runs the real `llama-server` against golden outputs.

### Changed

- The fork is now built on upstream v0.10.1 (it was v0.10.0). Upstream's changes in that release are described in its own release notes; where they meet the fork's additions, the entries under Fixed say what the fork does differently.
- The device ratings of all eight voice models were measured again on 2026-11-08. The machine was shared with other work, so most rows were taken at a one-minute load average of 1 to 6 instead of the quiet 1.0 or below; each model's highest load is recorded in the ratings file.
- Sidebar entries are renamed to "Transcription Models" and "Post-processing Models". The sidebar is wider and labels wrap instead of being truncated.
- The new sidebar entries Text-to-speech and Text-to-speech Models have their own icons, and a test keeps every sidebar icon unique.
- The Cargo project is now a workspace with two new crates, `handy-tts` (the voice worker) and `handy-tts-core` (shared text, scheduling, audio and download logic).
- The tray item "Unload Model" is renamed "Unload transcription model", and the tray model submenu is labelled "Transcription model".
- The transcription model dropdown in the footer is wider, shows each model's size, and lets long names wrap.
- Model buttons in the footer use the available width. A tooltip with the full name appears only when the name is still truncated.
- The post-processing shortcut is no longer listed on the Post-processing Models page.
- When post-processing is disabled, a cleared Raw Shortcut is restored to its default, and an assigned Raw Shortcut stays active.
- Retention cleanup of old history entries now announces each removed entry to the History page.
- Changing the post-processing provider, the local model or the compare view now saves with a checked write that restores the previous value on failure, and the tray refreshes after a provider or model change. Toggling an entry's saved status runs off the UI thread.
- Each helper program used to paste dictated text now runs under a deadline that grows with the length of the text, and its leftovers are cleaned up whatever the outcome, so a hung helper cannot hold up dictation, shortcuts or reading.
- One built-in pronunciation entry was removed from the technical reading vocabulary because splitting names in camelCase already reads that word correctly, and the bundled vocabulary examples now use generic paths.
- The built-in pronunciation vocabulary was audited on all eight voice models: every entry was read with and without its rule, three times each, and checked with speech-to-text. The dash and equals symbol entries are removed, because Handy already says "dash" and "equals" without them. The terms `src`, `kubectl`, `nginx` and `S1-mini` and every acronym except CLI, HTTP and UDP now apply only to the voices that need them, so for example Supertonic, Kokoro, Paradee, Phonon and Indic Tiny English read GPU as written and Pocket reads `nginx` as written. Pocket keeps every acronym spelled out, because reading acronyms as written changed how it spoke later sentences in the technical reading check. The Hindi and Punjabi voices, which spell Latin text letter by letter, keep every entry. All file extensions stay, because a built-in extension is also what makes a name such as `lib.Sh` read as a file name. Some bundled vocabulary examples now name the voice they check.
- In the Vocabulary tab, switching a voice's built-in entry back on when it was switched off for all voices, and the built-in list no longer has that entry for all voices, turns the all-voices switch into per-voice switches for the other voices that have the entry, so only that voice gets it back.

### Fixed

- Sidebar entries can be reached and opened with the keyboard.
- Borders, dividers, captions and rating tracks that used an unregistered colour now use the theme's colours in light and dark.
- The read-aloud popup follows the app theme, shows read text and its state in a readable grey, keeps the current sentence near the top without pulling you back while you scroll, and shows a focus ring on its buttons.
- Drop-down menus near the bottom of the window open upward instead of being cut off by the footer.
- Local post-processing is not cut off by upstream v0.10.1's 60-second post-processing deadline. That deadline now applies only to remote providers; the local model keeps its own limits (a start timeout and a per-chunk budget that grows with the transcript), so a long dictation is no longer pasted raw when its cleanup is still within budget.
- The PRIMARY selection helpers that upstream v0.10.1 added for Shift+Insert paste (`which` and `wl-copy`) run under the same deadlines as the rest of the paste path, so a helper that does not answer cannot hold up dictation. If the probe for them does not answer, the selection is skipped or reported as failed instead of being written through another route.
- With Shift+Insert paste on Wayland, the transcript and the highlighted text Handy puts back afterwards are passed to `wl-copy` on its input instead of its command line, where other local accounts could read them.
- A highlighted (PRIMARY) selection larger than a pipe buffer is no longer lost when dictating with Shift+Insert: it is read in full and put back. If it cannot be read at all, the transcript still goes on it so Shift+Insert pastes the dictation, and it is left there instead of being cleared.
- When pasting with a key combination on Linux, each helper tool is checked only when the ones before it did not send the keys, so a helper that does not answer no longer stops a paste that an earlier tool would have sent. A helper that does not answer its check still stops the paste with an error; a tool that is installed but fails to send hands over to the next one.
- Shortcut changes are applied atomically: a refused or failed change leaves the stored settings and registered shortcuts as they were, and an active dictation shortcut is always preserved.
- The History list keeps its loaded pages and scroll position when a dictation is added or updated, and when retention removes entries, instead of jumping back to the first page. A list that is already at the top stays at the top.
- Clear History also cancels any transcription still running for the entries it removes.
- The tray menu refreshes after post-processing is toggled, after the provider or local model changes, and after a native check click on a model entry.
- Selecting a model from the tray no longer blocks dictation.
- Selecting a different local model saves the selection before the running model is unloaded.
- Invisible characters in front of a think block no longer prevent the block from being removed from model output.
- A selected local model whose file is not downloaded no longer shows its name or a checkmark in the tray.
- The Prompts group is hidden for the local provider until the model list has loaded, so the prompt editor does not flash up for S1-mini.
- Supertonic on the Intel GPU no longer adds static (hiss above 12 kHz) to the audio. Its GPU vocoder is kept at full precision and its OpenVINO cache folder was renewed, so the first read on the GPU after updating prepares the device again.
- Pocket TTS on the Intel GPU no longer sounds too fast and unintelligible. Its flow sampler now runs on the CPU while the text conditioner stays on the GPU, which costs about 15% more CPU time.
- Starting a new read while the previous one is still being cancelled no longer shows "The text-to-speech worker stopped". A request the worker reports as busy is tried again every 25 ms for up to 10 seconds, and stops as soon as the read is cancelled. Failed worker replies are now logged with their cause.
- Characters that Supertonic has no sound for, such as box drawing, emoji and math symbols, are removed before synthesis, so pasted text with a table border no longer stops reading on the NPU. The spaces around them are collapsed.
- Changing Read code blocks or a technical reading setting while reading continues at the current position instead of repeating text that was already spoken.
- Kokoro read every diphthong and the sounds "j" and "ch" as two separate sounds, because the phonemizer's joined pairs were not mapped to the model's own symbols. They are now mapped, and repeated spaces in the phonemes collapse into one, which shortens the pauses. A piece of text with nothing to pronounce, such as "...", reads as a tenth of a second of silence instead of stopping the read with an error.

- Read-aloud Auto-delete choices of 3 days, 2 weeks and 3 months are saved; before, the app refused them.
- Deleting a dictation entry, a read-aloud or cleanup model, or resetting a vocabulary entry to its default now asks first, inline, and says what is removed.

- When the clipboard cannot be read, Read aloud says so instead of treating it as empty, which closed the popup or ended a recording.
- Read aloud refusals (not ready, busy pasting, clipboard not readable) appear on the floating overlay, on every press, when no dictation or dictation notice is using it and the read-aloud popup is closed. Opening the read-aloud popup hides a notice still on screen.

### Platform support

- Built and tested on Fedora 44 (KDE Plasma, Wayland) on an Intel Core Ultra 7 258V, and packaged as an RPM only.
- Local post-processing needs Unix sockets and uses the Linux parent-death signal to stop `llama-server` if the app dies. The `llama-server` bundling is Linux-only.
- Read aloud's worker process, its OpenVINO libraries, the parent-death signal and the 1 GiB free-memory check are Linux-only. On other platforms the memory check is skipped. Clipboard capture uses `wl-paste` or `xclip` on Linux.
- The device ratings shown on the Text-to-speech Models page were measured on that one machine. Other hardware may rank devices differently.
- Windows and macOS are not supported by these features yet.
